# Glass ornaments and felt decorations — product research

Checked on 12 November 2026. Machine-readable version of the same data: `product-candidates.json`.

## What this is, and what it is not

Every row below was read from the supplier's own product page on the day above: name, code, description, listed price, minimum and stock state. That makes the *listing* verified. It does not make the *product* verified: there is no quote, no sample, no compliance document and no permission to use any image. Nothing here is in the shop catalogue (`products.json`), and no price here is a retail price.

The brief asked for 30 products, 15 glass and 15 felt. The two suppliers that could be read yielded 15 glass listings and 8 felt listings. Eight of the glass listings are print variants of one logo bauble, so the real count is about 8 distinct glass products and 8 felt products. Padding the list to 30 would have meant inventing products.

## The main finding: these are wholesalers, not dropshippers

Both suppliers sell promotional goods to businesses. That changes the business model the shop was planned around.

- **Minimum quantities.** Logotrade lists a minimum per product (10 to 500 pieces). MP Reklaam lists a minimum order of 100 € and a single-piece price several times the bulk price (for example 18.10 € for one LED glass bauble, 3.62 € each from 20).
- **Stock has to be bought and held.** Neither site offers shipping single orders to the shop's customers. Selling these means buying a batch, storing it and posting parcels yourself. `CLAUDE.md` describes a dropshipping shop with a total budget of 100–300 €; one product at its minimum quantity can use all of that.
- **Origin.** Every MP Reklaam Christmas item read lists China as country of origin. The Logotrade glass baubles are described as made in Germany.
- **Several items are made to carry a logo.** The 8 cm glass baubles are priced with printing and have minimums of 240–500 pieces.

## Images

The mood pictures on the home page are AI-generated and must never stand in for a product.

## Pricing

No retail price is proposed, because the landed cost is unknown: delivery to the shop, packaging, postage to the customer, payment fees and VAT status are all missing. What can be said from the listed prices alone:

- A glass item bought at about 3–9 € without VAT would need a shelf price well above 10 € once 24 % VAT, parcel-machine postage and the unsold remainder of a batch are counted.
- Felt items at 0.72–5.58 € leave more room, and they do not break in the post.
- Glass in parcel machines needs protective packaging, which is a cost and a returns risk of its own.

`npm run validate` already computes net margin for any product in `products.json` once `supplier.unitCostEUR` and `supplier.shippingCostEUR` are filled in, and flags anything under 3 €.

## Recommendation

Start with felt, not glass, and with a handful of products rather than thirty.

1. Decide first whether the shop will hold stock. Without that, none of these can be sold.
2. If yes: ask both suppliers for a quote, delivery terms, the manufacturer's name and address, and permission to use their photos, for the in-stock felt items (gnome tree hanger, gnome decoration, gnome on sleigh, felt stocking).
3. Order one of each as a sample before listing anything.
4. Consider glass only after felt has sold, starting with the two in-stock packs of glass stars and bells.

## What the owner has to supply before any of these can go on sale

- the decision to buy and hold stock, and the budget for it
- a supplier quote per product: unit cost, delivery cost, delivery time
- manufacturer and EU responsible person details, product identifier and safety warnings for each product
- written permission for supplier photos, or own photos
- a retail price for each product
